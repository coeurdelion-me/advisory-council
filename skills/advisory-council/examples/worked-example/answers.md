# Answers to the panel's round 1 questions

*Synthetic example. Larkspur Cycles and every figure here are invented. The answers are deliberately
a mix of measured, estimated and unknown, as real ones are.*

Answers are attributed by seat and question number. All four seats receive this whole document.

## Answered

**Workshop time per subscriber (Strategist 4, Operator 1, Devil's Advocate 4, The Client 2).**
Measured over the last three months of job tickets: a puncture takes 25 minutes of labour on
average and a four-monthly service 55 minutes. Assuming 2 punctures a year and 3 services a year
per bike, one subscriber costs about 3.5 hours a year, so 28 bikes cost about 98 hours a year, or
close to 2 hours a week at full utilisation. The 6 hours a week of slack was measured in a quiet
October to December; the summer repair rush has no measured slack, and that is a gap.

**Theft rate (Strategist 1, Operator 4, Devil's Advocate 5, The Client 3).** No local data. The
partners' own assumption is 6 percent of the fleet a year (about 2 to 3 bikes of 40), replacement
cost 620 pounds each, so about 1,500 pounds a year. They have not asked an insurer for a quote.
That is a gap.

**Cash after the fleet (Operator 3, Devil's Advocate 3).** 30,000 pounds minus the fleet at 24,800
leaves 5,200 pounds. Advertising at 400 pounds a month for 12 months is 4,800 pounds, so the
advertising alone uses almost all of it before any replacement, parts or the first cancellations.
No month-by-month cash forecast exists.

**Employers (Strategist 5, Operator 5, Devil's Advocate 1).** One employer (about 60 staff) has
said "we'd love to"; the other has not been approached beyond a conversation. Nothing is signed.
Realistically month-12 utilisation without them is whatever the shop-window and social channels
deliver, and there is no estimate for that.

**Cannibalisation (Strategist 8, Operator 2, The Client 5).** Baseline retail sales of commuter
bikes are about 55 a year, repair revenue about 70,000 pounds a year. No threshold for "small
fraction" has been set. The partners guess 5 percent of retail sales would convert to subscriptions.

## Deliberately not answered

- **Evidence for 70 percent utilisation by month 12 (Strategist 6, The Client 1, Devil's
  Advocate 1).** There is none. The number is a target, not a forecast. This is the most valuable
  unanswered question: the whole case rests on it, so weight your verdict accordingly.
- **Contribution per bike-month by channel (Devil's Advocate 2).** Not computed. We would be
  inventing it.
- **Breakeven utilisation (The Client 4).** Same reason.
